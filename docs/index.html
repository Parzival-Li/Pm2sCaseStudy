Representative excerpts by metric

MUSTER PitchER: neighboring-pitch/octave substitutions in dense passages and possible mismatches

Observed across cases Of Prokofiev's 132 pitch records, 85 differ by one semitone and 37 by two; Ravel also has neighboring-pitch and octave differences. However, 3 of Haydn's 9 records involve ornament conversion despite identical principal pitches. Not every pitch record is an actual wrong note.

MUSTER MissRate: incomplete voice/chord preservation in rapid polyphonic passagework

Observed across cases A substantial lower arpeggio line in Ravel becomes rests. Prokofiev and Haydn also omit short notes at fast turns or phrase endings; Schubert locally loses chord members. The common observation is incomplete texture preservation, with different affected layers.

MUSTER ExtraRate: repeated or ornamental material expanded into additional short onsets

Local pattern; causes vary Schubert shows the strongest repeated short attacks; Haydn has extra onsets near ornaments, and Prokofiev adds subdivisions near the ending. Schubert's input MIDI already has 1,719 extra records against GT, versus 1,903 for predicted XML; these cannot all be attributed to model invention. Extra notes have no GT partner, so GT supplies nearby context only.

MUSTER OnsetER: inter-hand simultaneity and complex onset-grid changes

Observed across cases In this example, GT's ascending line and bass begin together; the prediction delays the bass by 1/4 quarter-note beat. Haydn and Ravel also regroup triplets and unequal tuplet groups. Only shift records are colored here. Full-piece OnsetER uses RCC and cannot be replaced by a shift-record count.

MUSTER OffsetER: changed voice endings and neighboring-onset normalization

Observed across cases Prokofiev frequently changes 1/2 to 1/4; Haydn changes dotted or ornament-bearing sustained notes. Ravel and Schubert alter the relationship between sustained voices and subdivided groups. Even identical raw durations can receive different normalized end positions.

MUSTER MeanER: a five-rate average, not a readability score

Aggregate metric; no separate note-error pattern Prokofiev has the lowest MeanER, 3.86925%, yet has 711 SS staff, 1,493 stem, and 450 spelling errors. MeanER has no independent erroneous-note category, so a separate 'MeanER error' coloring would be misleading.

SS miss: actual omissions mixed with onset-group/alignment residuals

Evaluation behavior across cases This window has 25 SS miss records, but 46 of its 47 GT pitches overlap the prediction as a pitch multiset. Ravel shows similar behavior. Miss counts therefore do not mean that the same number of sounding pitches are absent. Pitch overlap alone also establishes neither correct timing nor a basis for recomputing scores.

SS extra: added short onsets and unmatched substituted/regrouped notes

Evaluation behavior across cases Accompaniment lengths change and short attacks repeat; in other cases, semitone substitutions and changed chord groups also become SS extra. Schubert's saved-XML rerun increases miss and extra by 732 each while preserving their net difference, showing how strongly correspondence can affect counts.

SS duration: tuplet values and sustained-voice lengths reconstructed with other note values

Observed across cases; affected grids differ Haydn has 141 changes from 1/6 to 1/8; Schubert has 638 from 1/3 to 1/4 and 245 from 1/3 to 1/2. Prokofiev mainly changes 1/2 and 1/4 in either direction. The common issue is rhythm/length reconstruction, not a universal failure on all triplets. Missing notes also bypass subsequent duration comparison.

SS staff: reassignment of cross-hand/cross-staff voices

Observed across cases High chords move from GT's lower staff to the upper staff while the moving line moves downward. Haydn also shifts accompaniment between staves. Inappropriate retained clefs can create excessive ledger lines. Ravel has three GT staves versus two predicted staves, making staff scores unavailable rather than zero.

SS stem: polyphonic grouping/direction changes and legitimate engraving alternatives

Direction agreement; no general readability verdict Stem directions distinguishing sustained notes from moving lines are reorganized, also in Haydn and Schubert. Different directions need not be harder to read, and matching directions can remain difficult with unsuitable clefs. Stem disagreement is not uniformly classified as poor engraving here.

SS spell: enharmonic substitutions and missing tonal/harmonic spelling context

Observed across cases; severity varies greatly Ravel's seven-sharp framework is replaced with an enharmonic five-flat framework. Prokofiev also has D-flat to C-sharp and F-flat to E. These issues are much less frequent in Haydn and Schubert. Identical sounding pitches can still incur spelling errors; accidental visibility is a separate issue.

Evaluation scope and limitations

Cases were selected from 59 test performances and 14 GT pieces by MeanER, excluding repeated pieces. New examples use logs rerun from saved XML. Original-table and XML-rerun SS results differ substantially for Ravel and Schubert and remain separately reported. Default MUSTER omits Ravel's final 67 GT notes; with complete GT fixed, the between-performance MeanER gap is 11.0881 percentage points. Approximate playback alignment does not change metrics, correspondences, or original files.

The most supported next steps are to stabilize input/evaluation correspondence, then inspect polyphonic rhythm, sustained lengths, and texture preservation. Four cases do not establish a general causal claim such as pedal being the main cause of pitch errors.
